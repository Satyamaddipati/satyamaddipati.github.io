Case study

Reason-Guessr.

An interpretable image-geolocation system that predicts location from a single street-level image while surfacing the visual evidence behind the prediction.

Live demo ↗

01

Problem

Given a single street-level photo, predict where on Earth it was taken. Most geolocation models output a coordinate and nothing else — a recruiter or reviewer has no way to tell whether the model noticed the right things or got lucky on a correlated background feature.

02

Why this is hard

Two images from different continents can share similar lighting, road surfaces, or vegetation; two images from the same city can look completely different close-up versus from a distance. A model that only optimizes coordinate error has no incentive to ground its answer in anything a human could check.

03

Dataset

Evaluated on 32K images across 8,000 locations. Each location contributes multiple images so the model can't just memorize a single viewpoint per place.

04

Architecture

A CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder turns the image into a visual embedding. That embedding is fused with a set of interpretable cues before a regression head predicts latitude and longitude — so the final coordinate is traceable back to specific visual evidence, not just a black-box embedding.

05

Interpretable cues

  • Climate and vegetation
  • License plates
  • Architecture style

Surfacing these cues means a reviewer can sanity-check a prediction against the same evidence the model used, instead of trusting a single opaque number.

06

Experiments

90.6 kmCLIP embedding alone
88.9 kmwith cue fusion

Median haversine error. Cue fusion gives a modest, consistent improvement over the embedding-only baseline — not a dramatic one, which is itself informative about how much of the signal CLIP's embedding already captures on its own.

07

Limitations

The hardest cases are locations with few distinguishing visual cues — similar climate zones, no visible signage, generic architecture. Cue fusion narrows the error on average; it doesn't remove this category of ambiguity, and an honest accounting of the result has to say so rather than imply the system resolves every case.

08

What I'd try next

The small gap between the embedding-only and cue-fusion numbers suggests the interpretable cues are currently acting more as a sanity check than a strong independent signal. A natural next step is weighting cues by how confidently each one fires, rather than fusing them uniformly, so a strong plate match can outvote a weak vegetation guess.
